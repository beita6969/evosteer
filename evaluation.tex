\section{Authority and scope}

This document freezes the benchmark science for
\texttt{skillev-benchmark-protocol@10}.  The learning method remains governed
exclusively by \texttt{idea.tex}; this document fixes only experimental
populations, terminal measurements, reward projections, and comparison rules.
The machine-readable projection is
\texttt{configs/evaluation/protocol\_v10.yaml}.  A formal run is inadmissible
until the runtime implements that projection exactly.

All comparisons use one seed, 0, and the same ordered tasks, budgets, model
revision, tokenizer, evaluator versions, and initial skill library.  The three
first required conditions are exact SkillFlow baseline,
BayesianImprove-full, and BayesianImprove-no-calibration.  The latter two are
not aliases for the historical \texttt{SKILLFLOW\_DISABLED} arm.

\section{Population isolation}

Every domain has three disjoint roles: training, progress validation, and
final evaluation.  Final evaluation items are never sampled for training,
posterior updates, threshold selection, skill authoring, or prompt examples.
Final evaluation is read-only: it cannot update model parameters, the
Beta--Bernoulli posterior, detector state, or the skill library.

Isolation is checked using canonical source identifiers and normalized public
task content.  Code tasks additionally compare public function signatures;
spreadsheet tasks compare instructions, workbook structure, and formulas;
interactive tasks compare goal, scenario, and environment identifiers.  Any
overlap aborts the experiment.  Reference answers, tests, rubric criteria,
hidden environment state, and per-item reports remain inside the trusted
evaluator and never enter model context or Git.

The training mixture follows the SkillFlow construction rule: every benchmark
contributes exactly 512 episodes, for 4,608 episodes in total.  The benchmark
order in this document defines construction and tie-breaking only.  Within
each benchmark, source tasks are deterministically shuffled with seed 0.  If a
source population has fewer than 512 unique tasks, additional independently
shuffled cycles are appended until the benchmark contributes 512 episodes;
every occurrence receives a distinct episode ID while retaining its
source-task ID.  If the population has at least 512 tasks, the first 512 of the
frozen shuffled order are used.  The nine equal-size selections are then
combined and globally shuffled with seed 0, matching SkillFlow's mixed-domain
training data rather than presenting nine consecutive domain blocks.  The
complete 4,608-episode order is shared by every method and never adapted from
reward or accuracy.

This 512-per-domain rule applies only to the training mixture.  Validation and
final-evaluation populations retain the independent sizes specified below and
are never filled with repeated training items.

\section{Terminal signal contract}

Each completed evaluator call emits native metrics, a TTB reward
$R(\tau)\in[0,1]$, and a separate binary posterior outcome
$Y(\tau)\in\{0,1\}$.  The TTB objective consumes only $R$; the
Beta--Bernoulli update consumes only $Y$.  Continuous metrics are therefore
not silently treated as Bernoulli observations.

Infrastructure failure is not a zero reward.  It aborts the uncommitted step.
A completed evaluation of a legal but incorrect or invalid candidate is a
valid zero reward and remains in the batch.  Native private payloads are
log-only evaluator evidence and are not observations for the evaluated model.

The HealthBench threshold is preregistered at 0.60 and cannot be tuned on
HealthBench.  Its second conjunct prevents a high positive-point total from
masking any triggered negative-weight rubric criterion.  The official raw
per-example score may be negative; only the TTB projection clips it to
$[0,1]$.  Public reporting follows the official aggregate rather than the
clipped training reward.

\section{Frozen benchmark populations}

The per-domain selections are constructed in this fixed order before the
global seed-0 shuffle:

\begin{center}
HotpotQA $\rightarrow$ TriviaQA $\rightarrow$ AIME training $\rightarrow$
Health training $\rightarrow$ WebShop $\rightarrow$ ALFWorld $\rightarrow$
Spreadsheet training $\rightarrow$ AppWorld $\rightarrow$ code training.
\end{center}

Each domain contributes exactly 512 episodes under the repeat-and-reshuffle rule
above.  The names AIME 2026, HealthBench, SpreadsheetBench, and MBPP+ identify
capability domains in the training mixture; their protected final populations
themselves remain evaluation-only.

\subsection{HotpotQA}

Use HotpotQA v1.1 distractor mode and its official evaluator.  Training and
progress validation are a fixed disjoint partition of the official training
population.  Final evaluation is the complete official distractor development
population.  Report answer F1 and EM.  Supporting-fact labels remain private
and are not added to model context.

\subsection{TriviaQA}

Use TriviaQA v1.0 unfiltered.  Training and progress validation are a fixed
disjoint partition of the official training population; final evaluation is
the complete official development population.  Use the official normalization
and report answer F1 and EM.

\subsection{AIME 2026}

All thirty AIME 2026 tasks are final-evaluation-only.  Train on AIME
1983--2024 and use AIME 2025 as progress validation.  Exact problem identity
and normalized statement overlap are forbidden across years and sources.
The final answer is graded by the official symbolic-equivalence path and
reported as accuracy.

\subsection{HealthBench}

The primary final population is the complete 5,000-example OpenAI HealthBench
release dated 7 May 2025.  HealthBench Hard and HealthBench Consensus are not
additional primary populations in protocol 10.  Training and validation use
independently licensed health-dialogue and safety tasks with no HealthBench
conversation, rubric, or reference response.  Before the first formal run,
Protocol 10 amendment \texttt{protocol-v10-healthbench-qwen-judge@1} replaced
the unused GPT-4.1 profile with the frozen Qwen3.5-9B base model served by
SGLang at temperature zero.  The official rubric-item prompt and score formula
remain unchanged.  The judge never loads a training adapter.  These results
are reported as ``HealthBench, Qwen3.5-9B judge'' and are not claimed to be
official GPT-4.1-graded HealthBench scores.  Malformed judge output or grader
failure is infrastructure failure, never reward zero.

\subsection{WebShop}

Freeze three disjoint goal-ID partitions from the official 12,087-instruction
release before training.  Training and validation use their respective
partitions; the held-out partition is final evaluation.  Report average native
score and native success rate.  Product data and environment build are the
same across compared methods.

\subsection{ALFWorld}

Training uses official train games and progress validation uses a fixed
holdout from train.  Final evaluation uses the complete
\texttt{valid\_seen} and \texttt{valid\_unseen} populations from data release
2.1.1.  Report their success rates separately; a combined rate may be shown
only as an explicitly secondary statistic.

\subsection{SpreadsheetBench}

The sole primary final population is SpreadsheetBench V1 Verified, the
expert-annotated 400-instance release.  Neither the original 912-instance set
nor SpreadsheetBench 2 is silently substituted.  Training and validation use
independently generated spreadsheet tasks whose instructions, workbooks,
formula graphs, and test cases do not overlap the official population.  The
official online-judge-style evaluator runs every workbook case; Pass@1 requires
all cases for the instruction to pass.

\subsection{AppWorld}

Use the official data release 0.2.0.  Training uses \texttt{train}, progress
validation uses \texttt{dev}, and final evaluation reports
\texttt{test\_normal} and \texttt{test\_challenge} separately and jointly.
Per-task TGC is the continuous reward.  SGC is computed only by the official
offline evaluator after every task variation in a scenario group is present;
it is never fabricated as an episode metric.

\subsection{MBPP+ fixed-100}

EvalPlus v0.2.0 contains 378 MBPP+ tasks and publishes no canonical
``hard-100'' population.  Protocol 10 therefore uses the honest identity
\texttt{mbpp-plus-fixed-100@1}: the first 100 tasks in ascending canonical
EvalPlus task-ID order.  The list is materialized before any project-model
result is run.  It must not be described as hard-100.  Training and validation
use independent code tasks and exclude all 378 MBPP+ prompts and public
signatures.  Report official EvalPlus pass@1.

\section{Reporting and comparison}

The final report includes all native metrics in the table, record counts,
infrastructure failures, parsing failures, wall time, model-call and token
cost, and the identities of the method, model, tokenizer, evaluator, and each
population.  AppWorld includes both task and complete-scenario counts.
ALFWorld keeps seen and unseen results separate.  HealthBench identifies the
grader snapshot and also reports the binary success rate defined above.

Only completed, equally budgeted conditions enter method comparisons.  Missing
conditions remain visible; they are not imputed.  One-seed results are called
registered primary-seed evidence and not multi-seed robustness evidence.
